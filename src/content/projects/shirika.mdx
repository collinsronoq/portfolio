## Overview

The Court Management System is a web-based financial and administrative platform designed for residential courts, estates, and gated communities.

The system digitizes the management of monthly service fees, household balances, and expenditure records while providing structured governance workflows for administrators and committee members.

It replaces informal spreadsheets, paper ledgers, and fragmented communication channels with a centralized and auditable platform.

---

## Problem

Many residential courts operate using manual or semi-digital systems (Excel sheets, WhatsApp updates, handwritten receipts). These approaches introduce structural weaknesses:

### 1. Limited Transparency in Spending
- Members often lack visibility into how service fees are spent.
- Delays in publishing expenditure reports lead to mistrust.
- No standardized approval workflow for major expenses.
- Difficulty verifying receipts or expenditure breakdowns.

### 2. Disputes Over Contributions and Balances
- Manual record-keeping leads to reconciliation errors.
- Households are unsure of their current balance status.
- Historical contribution records are difficult to retrieve.
- Committee transitions cause data loss or inconsistencies.

### 3. Inefficient Payment Workflows
- Payments are made through bank transfers or mobile money without proper tracking.
- Manual confirmation of payments consumes administrative time.
- No real-time balance updates for households.
- Risk of unrecorded or misallocated payments.

### 4. Weak Audit Trails
- No structured logs for edits or deletions.
- Lack of accountability when financial records change.
- No exportable financial summaries for AGMs or reporting.

### 5. Administrative Bottlenecks
- Treasurers handle most processes manually.
- Generating monthly summaries is time-consuming.
- Reporting requires aggregation from multiple sources.

---

## Solution

The Court Management System addresses these challenges through structured financial workflows and role-based controls.

### Contribution Management
- Monthly service fee configuration per household.
- Automated balance calculation (arrears and overpayments).
- Historical contribution ledger per unit.
- Real-time balance visibility for each house.

### Transparent Expenditure Tracking
- Categorized expenditure recording (security, garbage collection, maintenance, utilities, etc.).
- Attachment of digital receipts or references.
- Structured approval flow for large expenditures.
- Auto-generated expenditure summaries for reporting periods.

### Integrated Payment Workflow
- Payment initiation directly from the application.
- Automated payment confirmation and reconciliation.
- Immediate balance updates upon successful transactions.
- Reduced administrative overhead for treasurers.

### Reporting & Dashboards
- Financial overview dashboard (total collected, total spent, net balance).
- Household-level account statements.
- Monthly and annual financial summaries.
- Exportable reports for AGMs and committee reviews.

### Governance & Role-Based Access
- Role separation (Admin, Treasurer, Member).
- Restricted financial modification rights.
- Controlled access to sensitive financial data.
- Structured audit trail for accountability.

## Architecture Notes

### Backend
- **FastAPI** for API and server-rendered workflows.
- Domain-driven route separation (contributions, expenditures, households, authentication).
- Modular service layer for financial calculations.

### Database Design
- **SQLAlchemy ORM** for structured relational modeling.
- Normalized financial tables (Household, Contribution, Expenditure, Payment).
- Deterministic balance computation (ledger-based, not manually stored).
- Indexed transaction tables for reporting efficiency.

### Authentication & Authorization
- Secure login system.
- Role-based permission enforcement at route and service layer.
- Controlled write-access to financial operations.

### Reporting Logic
- Aggregation queries for monthly totals.
- Derived balance fields computed from transaction history.
- Clean separation between transactional data and reporting views.

## Impact

- Reduced disputes over balances and arrears.
- Streamlined monthly reporting process.

---

## Key Design Decisions

- Ledger-based financial modeling instead of manually editable balances.
- Strict separation of contribution and expenditure domains.
- Emphasis on auditability over convenience.
- Minimalistic but functional administrative dashboard.

---

## Learnings

### Financial Data Modeling
Designing ledger-style systems requires:
- Avoiding duplicated balance fields.
- Ensuring transactional integrity.
- Structuring queries for performance and clarity.

### Governance-Oriented Software Design
Community systems are socio-technical:
- Transparency features are as important as technical correctness.
- Role separation prevents misuse.
- Clear reporting structures build user trust.

### Workflow Design
Real-world treasurer workflows informed:
- Contribution reconciliation logic.
- Expenditure categorization structure.
- Dashboard metrics prioritization.
